Getting started

Install AutoChase, connect your Jira projects, define SLAs, configure organisation structure, and set up capacity planning.

  1. Install AutoChase from the Atlassian Marketplace.
  2. Open Jira → Apps → AutoChase (or Manage apps → AutoChase).
  3. Add Projects from Jira and assign default SLAs and teams.
  4. Create SLA rules with response/resolution times, reminders, leadership visibility, and working hours.
  5. Open Organisation and add at least one department, team, and escalation hierarchy record.
  6. Open Capacity planning → Setup (gear icon). Configure estimate source, default hours, and story points if needed → Save settings.
  7. Use Capacity planning → Availability for leave, recurring meetings, and per-person overrides.
  8. Review Sprint commitment and Backlog pressure to see workload vs capacity.

Navigation in the app: Dashboard · Tracked issues · Reports · Capacity planning · Projects · SLA rules · Organisation

A setup reminder appears on Dashboard and Reports until department, team, and escalation hierarchy are configured. Team reports and manager-chain escalations require this org data.

Capacity planning

See open Jira work against each person’s capacity. AutoChase combines SLA deadlines with workload so you can spot overload before breach.

Capacity planning uses six sections (sub-tabs):

Sprint commitment Backlog pressure Reports Velocity Availability Setup

Sprint commitment

Plan a specific Jira sprint: compare demand (assigned work in the sprint) to supply (available hours after meetings, leave, and focus factor).

FilterDescription
BoardJira Scrum/Kanban board — sprint dates and sprint-scoped work come from this board
SprintActive sprint or a specific sprint from the board
View byPerson (capacity and overload per assignee) or Project (workload rollup)
SearchFilter rows by person or project name

Summary KPIs: Demand (h), Supply (h), Utilization %, Gap (h), Overloaded count.

Positive gap means more work is assigned than the team can deliver in the sprint window. Sprint commitment is board-scoped — pick the board that matches how your team plans sprints.

Backlog pressure

Open workload vs weekly capacity (not sprint-length). Use this for ongoing backlog health.

BandOK (<85%), High (85–100%), Overload (>100%)

Filters: projects, group by (person or project), time window (All open, Sprint, Outside active sprint, Updated this month/year), utilization band, and search. Per overloaded person, use Set Xh/wk to apply a recommended weekly hours override.

Velocity

Throughput trends from closed sprints on a Jira board. Use for forecasting and comparing team members.

  • Board — velocity is read from closed sprints on this board
  • Sprints — recent closed (last 3/6/9/12) or pick specific closed sprints
  • Chart — hours or story points
  • Compare people — optional overlay lines on the trend chart

Click Apply after changing filters. Charts show team throughput; tables show per-person velocity summary and sprint-by-sprint breakdown. An upcoming sprint forecast uses historical velocity when board data is available.

Availability

  • Leave — personal leave periods reduce sprint supply
  • Recurring meetings — templates and per-person meeting blocks
  • Person overrides — custom weekly capacity or meeting hours for one person
  • Focus factor — team-wide share of capacity for focused sprint work (configured in Setup)

Click Save settings after changes, then Refresh on the relevant capacity tab.

How demand is calculated

  1. Uses Remaining estimate (or Original estimate / Story points per your setting)
  2. Per-project overrides apply when configured
  3. If time estimate is missing, uses minutes if missing or story points × hours/point
  4. Unassigned issues can appear in a separate unassigned pool

Capacity on the issue panel

SLA rules

Define response and resolution targets, reminders, leadership visibility, SLA clock behavior, and working hours.

SLA modes

  • Single SLA — same response/resolution for all priorities
  • Priority-based SLA (P1–P5) — different targets per priority

Five-step wizard

  1. SLA Timing — name, times, warning threshold, scope, SLA clock, working hours
  2. Acknowledgment reminders — up to 3 percentages of response time
  3. Progress reminders — up to 3 percentages of resolution time
  4. Leadership visibility levels — manager-chain thresholds
  5. Review & submit

Key fields

FieldPurpose
Response / Resolution timeSLA targets
Warning threshold (%)When status becomes At risk
Clock startsJira created, on status match, or on active sprint
Pause while status is…Statuses that stop the SLA clock
Working timeCount SLA elapsed time only during business hours (see Working calendar)

Status lifecycle

On track → At risk (warning %) → Breach (resolution exceeded)

Working calendar

Configure when SLA time counts — business hours, weekends, short days, holidays, and timezone.

Configure working time in SLA rules → Create/Edit SLA → Step 1 → Working time.

Enable business hours

Check Count this SLA only during working hours. When enabled, SLA budgets (e.g. 16 hours) advance only during configured windows. Nights, unchecked days, and holidays do not count.

Timezone

Searchable IANA timezone picker. Use Use my timezone for a quick start. All day/time rules apply in this timezone.

Work days and hours

Same hours every day

  • Check Mon–Fri (default); uncheck Sat and Sun to exclude weekends
  • Set Start (e.g. 09:00) and End (e.g. 17:00)

Different hours per day

Enable Set different hours for each day. Example: Mon–Thu 09:00–17:00, Friday 09:00–13:00 (half day).

Holidays

  • Enter dates as YYYY-MM-DD (one per line)
  • Upload CSV, TSV, or Excel (.xlsx)
  • Use presets: US federal, UK bank, India public, EU common, APAC mix (2026)
  • Download template for bulk import
Example: Mon–Fri 09:00–17:00, Friday 09:00–13:00, Sat/Sun off, plus regional holiday list — a 16-hour SLA counts only business minutes in that calendar.

Organisation

Departments, teams, and escalation hierarchy in one place.

Departments Teams Escalation hierarchy

Departments

Organizational buckets for analytics and escalation context. Add name and optional description. Create departments before teams.

Teams

Group people and projects under a department. Assign teams to projects from the Projects tab. Used by Reports team filter and org columns on issues.

Escalation hierarchy

Upload org structure for manager-chain leadership visibility.

  1. Download template (columns: employee_id, name, email, reports_to_id)
  2. Import CSV, TSV, or Excel (.xlsx)
  3. Click Resolve Jira IDs to link emails to Jira accounts
  4. Verify in the table; use Add employee for manual edits

Leadership visibility levels in SLA rules walk the manager chain from the assignee using this hierarchy.

How it works

End-to-end flow once projects, SLAs, organisation, and capacity are configured.

  1. Tracking — New issues in enabled projects are tracked (via webhook and sync)
  2. SLA assignment — From project default and SLA matching rules; manual override per issue
  3. SLA clock — Runs during working hours if configured; pauses on selected statuses
  4. Status — on_track → at_risk → breach
  5. Reminders — Acknowledgment and progress comments at configured thresholds
  6. Leadership visibility — Escalation levels via hierarchy on scheduled checks
  7. Capacity — Workload from open issues; compared to weekly hours, sprint supply, and SLA time remaining
  8. Resolution — Tracking stops when issue reaches done/resolved
